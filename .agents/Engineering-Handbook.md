# Preface

The **Socrato Engineering Handbook** is the primary engineering and product reference for the Socrato platform.

It defines the long-term vision, educational philosophy, engineering standards, architectural principles, and development practices that guide every aspect of the project.

This handbook exists to ensure that Socrato evolves as a coherent, maintainable, secure, and educationally meaningful platform. Whether a feature is designed by a human developer or generated by an AI coding agent, every implementation should remain aligned with the principles described in this document.

The handbook intentionally focuses on **why** the platform is built the way it is and **which principles** should guide every decision.

Implementation details—including APIs, database schemas, business rules, workflows, and feature specifications—are documented separately in the **Socrato Technical Specifications**.

Whenever uncertainty exists, the principles described in this handbook always take precedence over implementation convenience.

---

# About This Handbook

This handbook is intended to remain relatively stable over time.

It documents the permanent principles that define Socrato rather than the implementation details of individual features.

As the platform evolves:

- the **Engineering Handbook** defines **why** the platform is built this way;
- the **Technical Specifications** define **how** each feature is implemented.

Every contributor—including software engineers, designers, product managers, and AI coding agents—should consult this handbook before making significant product, architectural, or engineering decisions.

# Part I — Product Foundation

# 1. Vision

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Socrato exists to help students learn more effectively while empowering teachers through responsible, privacy-first artificial intelligence.

---

## Purpose

Socrato is an AI-powered tutoring platform designed to help Québec secondary school students master History through adaptive Socratic dialogue while providing teachers with meaningful insight into student learning and progress.

The platform combines educational expertise with artificial intelligence to deliver personalized learning experiences that strengthen understanding, critical thinking, and long-term knowledge retention.

---

## Product Vision

Our ambition extends beyond building educational software.

Socrato aims to become a trusted learning companion that supports every student in developing deeper understanding, stronger reasoning skills, and greater confidence, while enabling teachers to personalize instruction at scale without increasing their workload.

Artificial intelligence is **not** the purpose of Socrato.

It is the technology that enables a more human, more personalized, and more effective educational experience.

---

## Mission

Socrato exists to:

- help students think rather than memorize;
- encourage independent learning;
- provide adaptive and personalized guidance;
- reinforce classroom instruction;
- respect teacher expertise;
- preserve student privacy;
- support long-term educational success.

---

## Long-Term Vision

The long-term objective of Socrato is to become the world's most trusted AI learning companion for education.

The platform should continuously evolve while remaining faithful to three permanent commitments:

- educational excellence;
- teacher empowerment;
- responsible use of artificial intelligence.

Every major product evolution should strengthen—not dilute—these commitments.

---

## Design Philosophy

Every decision made throughout the platform should reinforce the same fundamental idea:

> AI should make education **more human**, not more automated.

Technology should never become the center of the learning experience.

Students, teachers, and learning always come first.

---

## Engineering Implications

This vision influences every aspect of the platform.

All architectural, product, engineering, design, and AI decisions should contribute to:

- improving learning outcomes;
- increasing student engagement;
- supporting teacher decision-making;
- protecting privacy by default;
- ensuring long-term sustainability;
- maintaining simplicity and clarity.

---

## Vision Check

Whenever uncertainty exists, every contributor should ask one question before making a decision:

> **Will this decision help students learn more effectively while strengthening the role of the teacher?**

# 2. Core Principles

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Every decision made throughout the development of Socrato should remain aligned with the following foundational principles.

---

## Learning First

Learning is the primary purpose of Socrato.

Educational value always takes precedence over technology, convenience, visual appeal, or novelty. Every feature should demonstrably improve the learning experience.

**Engineering Implication**

When multiple implementation options exist, choose the one that provides the greatest educational benefit.

---

## Privacy First

Student privacy is fundamental.

Only the minimum amount of information required to support learning should be collected, processed, or stored. Privacy must be considered from the earliest stages of product design.

**Engineering Implication**

Every feature should follow the principles of data minimization, anonymization, and secure data handling.

---

## Teacher First

Teachers remain the educational experts.

Socrato is designed to extend the teacher's ability to personalize learning—not replace professional judgment or instructional expertise.

**Engineering Implication**

Teachers should always retain control over educational decisions, learning objectives, and classroom activities.

---

## AI as a Tutor

Artificial intelligence exists to guide thinking—not provide shortcuts.

The AI should encourage reasoning, curiosity, reflection, and independent learning through adaptive Socratic dialogue.

**Engineering Implication**

AI responses should prioritize guidance, questioning, and feedback over direct answers whenever educationally appropriate.

---

## Simple by Default

Simplicity improves learning.

Interfaces, workflows, and engineering solutions should eliminate unnecessary complexity while preserving flexibility where needed.

**Engineering Implication**

Choose the simplest solution that fully satisfies the educational objective.

---

## Build for the Long Term

Socrato is intended to evolve over many years.

Every architectural, engineering, and product decision should prioritize maintainability, scalability, reliability, and long-term sustainability over short-term convenience.

**Engineering Implication**

Avoid decisions that create unnecessary technical debt or limit future evolution.

---

## Principle Check

Before implementing any feature, ask:

- Does it improve learning?
- Does it respect student privacy?
- Does it empower teachers?
- Does it use AI responsibly?
- Is it as simple as possible?
- Will it remain maintainable over time?

If the answer to any of these questions is **No**, the proposed solution should be reconsidered.

# 3. Product Philosophy

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Every product decision should begin with one question:
>
> **Does this help students learn better?**

---

## Learning Before Technology

Technology is not the product.

Learning is.

Artificial intelligence, software, and automation exist only to improve educational outcomes. A feature that does not create meaningful educational value does not belong in Socrato.

**Product Principle**

Educational impact always takes priority over technological innovation.

---

## Guide, Don't Replace

Socrato is a tutor—not an answer machine.

Rather than delivering immediate solutions, the platform encourages students to think, reason, question, and gradually discover answers through guided learning.

The objective is not to make learning easier.

The objective is to make learning deeper.

---

## Progress Over Performance

Learning is measured by growth—not by speed.

The platform values:

- understanding over memorization;
- confidence over perfection;
- long-term mastery over short-term performance;
- continuous improvement over isolated results.

Students should feel encouraged to improve rather than pressured to achieve perfect scores.

---

## Teachers Stay in Control

Teachers remain at the center of the educational process.

Socrato complements classroom instruction by providing personalized support while respecting the teacher's expertise, instructional choices, and educational objectives.

Artificial intelligence supports teaching.

It never replaces it.

---

## Personalization With Purpose

Every learner progresses differently.

Socrato continuously adapts its guidance according to each student's:

- demonstrated understanding;
- misconceptions;
- learning pace;
- confidence;
- previous progress.

Personalization should always serve learning—not simply create variety.

---

## Privacy by Design

Trust is essential for learning.

Student information should be collected only when it provides clear educational value and should always be protected through responsible engineering practices.

Privacy is not an additional feature.

It is a permanent design principle.

---

## Simplicity by Default

Learning should never compete with the interface.

Every workflow should remain intuitive, focused, and free from unnecessary complexity.

When multiple designs satisfy the same educational objective, the simplest solution should be preferred.

---

## Build Trust

Students and teachers should always understand what the platform is doing and why.

Socrato should remain:

- transparent;
- predictable;
- respectful;
- supportive;
- trustworthy.

Trust is earned through consistency—not complexity.

---

## Product Philosophy Check

Before introducing any new feature, ask:

- Does it improve learning?
- Does it encourage thinking?
- Does it strengthen the teacher's role?
- Does it respect student privacy?
- Does it keep the experience simple?
- Will it increase users' trust in the platform?

If the answer is **No**, the feature should be redesigned or reconsidered.

# 4. Pedagogical Principles

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Socrato is designed to develop understanding—not simply produce correct answers.

---

## Learning Through Inquiry

Socrato follows the Socratic method.

Rather than immediately providing answers, the AI guides students through carefully sequenced questions that stimulate reasoning, reflection, and discovery.

The objective is to help students construct knowledge themselves.

---

## Understanding Before Memorization

True learning begins with comprehension.

Students should first understand concepts, relationships, historical reasoning, and cause-and-effect before attempting to memorize facts or dates.

Memorization should reinforce understanding—not replace it.

---

## Active Learning

Learning is most effective when students actively participate.

Activities should encourage learners to:

- analyze;
- interpret;
- explain;
- justify;
- compare;
- evaluate;
- apply knowledge.

Passive consumption of information should be minimized.

---

## Productive Struggle

Learning requires intellectual effort.

Socrato should provide enough guidance for students to succeed while preserving the cognitive challenge necessary for meaningful learning.

The platform should remove frustration—not thinking.

---

## Scaffolded Learning

Support should be delivered progressively.

Hints, guiding questions, explanations, and examples should become increasingly explicit only when necessary.

Whenever possible, students should solve problems independently before receiving additional assistance.

---

## Personalized Learning

Every learner progresses differently.

Socrato continuously adapts instruction according to each student's:

- demonstrated understanding;
- misconceptions;
- learning pace;
- confidence;
- historical progress.

Adaptation should always serve educational goals rather than simply changing question difficulty.

---

## Immediate and Meaningful Feedback

Feedback should be:

- timely;
- specific;
- constructive;
- actionable.

Rather than simply identifying mistakes, Socrato should explain why an answer is incomplete and guide students toward a deeper understanding.

---

## Retrieval Practice

Long-term memory is strengthened through active recall.

Students should regularly retrieve previously learned concepts instead of relying solely on repeated explanations or passive review.

Retrieval should become a natural part of every learning journey.

---

## Mastery Through Practice

Learning develops through repeated application over time.

Students should revisit concepts until they consistently demonstrate understanding rather than simply completing a predetermined number of activities.

Progress is measured by mastery—not completion.

---

## Metacognition

Students should gradually become aware of their own learning.

Socrato should encourage learners to reflect on:

- what they understand;
- what remains difficult;
- why mistakes occurred;
- which learning strategies help them improve.

Helping students learn **how to learn** is one of the platform's long-term objectives.

---

## Growth Mindset

Mistakes are valuable learning opportunities.

The AI should reinforce:

- perseverance;
- curiosity;
- confidence;
- resilience;
- continuous improvement.

Success should be presented as the result of sustained effort rather than innate ability.

---

## Authentic Learning

Whenever possible, learning activities should reflect authentic educational situations.

Students should interpret historical evidence, analyze documents, justify conclusions, and make informed decisions rather than memorize isolated facts.

Learning should resemble real historical thinking.

---

## Teacher Partnership

Teachers remain the educational experts.

Socrato extends classroom instruction through personalized tutoring while remaining fully aligned with each teacher's objectives and instructional decisions.

The platform should strengthen—not replace—the teacher-student relationship.

---

## Continuous Improvement

Learning is cumulative.

Each tutoring session should build upon previous learning so that students gradually develop long-term understanding instead of isolated knowledge.

Every interaction should contribute to the learner's overall progression.

---

## Pedagogical Check

Before implementing any educational feature, ask:

- Does it encourage thinking?
- Does it deepen understanding?
- Does it promote independent learning?
- Does it adapt to the learner?
- Does it strengthen long-term retention?
- Does it support the teacher's educational objectives?

If the answer is **No**, the feature should be reconsidered.

# 5. User Experience Principles

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> The interface should disappear behind the learning experience.
>
> Users should focus on learning—not on figuring out how to use the platform.

---

## Learning First

Every screen, interaction, and component should support a clear educational objective.

Visual design, animations, and interface elements exist to facilitate learning—not to attract attention.

Whenever a design decision competes with educational effectiveness, learning always takes priority.

---

## Clarity Over Complexity

Users should see only what they need, when they need it.

Interfaces should eliminate unnecessary information, competing actions, and visual distractions.

A clear interface reduces cognitive load and helps students remain focused on learning.

---

## Consistency Builds Confidence

Consistency reduces uncertainty.

Navigation patterns, layouts, terminology, icons, buttons, and interactions should behave predictably throughout the platform.

Users should never have to relearn how Socrato works from one screen to another.

---

## Progressive Disclosure

Complexity should appear only when it becomes necessary.

Advanced functionality should remain accessible without overwhelming first-time users.

The interface should reveal information progressively as users move through their learning journey.

---

## Accessibility by Default

Socrato should be usable by the widest possible audience.

Every interface should consider accessibility from the beginning, including:

- readable typography;
- sufficient color contrast;
- keyboard navigation;
- responsive layouts;
- assistive technologies;
- clear interaction feedback.

Accessibility is a design requirement—not an optional enhancement.

---

## Responsive by Design

Every device deserves its own optimized experience.

Mobile interfaces should be intentionally designed rather than simply scaled down from desktop layouts.

Students should experience the same quality of learning regardless of the device they use.

---

## Immediate Feedback

Every user action should produce timely and meaningful feedback.

Users should always understand:

- what happened;
- what is happening;
- what they should do next.

Clear feedback builds confidence and reduces uncertainty.

---

## Minimal Friction

Every unnecessary click, confirmation, or interruption increases cognitive load.

Workflows should feel natural, efficient, and uninterrupted.

The platform should never create obstacles between students and learning.

---

## Support Without Interrupting

Guidance should remain available without becoming intrusive.

Hints, recommendations, and contextual assistance should help users progress while preserving concentration and learning flow.

Support should empower—not distract.

---

## Delight Through Simplicity

A polished interface builds trust.

Good design is not decorative.

Good design makes learning feel natural, effortless, and enjoyable.

Visual quality should reinforce clarity—not compete with it.

---

## User Experience Check

Before introducing or modifying an interface, ask:

- Is the educational objective immediately clear?
- Does the interface reduce cognitive load?
- Can users predict what will happen next?
- Is the workflow as simple as possible?
- Is the design consistent with the rest of the platform?
- Does the interface help users stay focused on learning?

If the answer is **No**, the experience should be simplified before implementation.

# 6. Navigation Flows

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Navigation exists to support learning.
>
> Users should always know where they are, what they are doing, and what they should do next.

---

## Navigation Philosophy

Navigation is the framework that connects every learning experience within Socrato.

Every screen, interaction, and workflow should contribute to a simple, predictable, and goal-oriented experience.

The platform is built around two primary user journeys:

- the **Teacher Journey**;
- the **Student Journey**.

Secondary workflows should naturally integrate into these journeys without increasing unnecessary complexity.

---

# Teacher Journey

The teacher experience focuses on planning instruction, creating learning activities, monitoring student progress, and adapting future teaching.

## Primary Workflow

```text
Landing Page
    ↓
Teacher Authentication
    ↓
Teacher Dashboard
    ↓
Create or Select a Group
    ↓
Import or Manage Students
    ↓
Create a Practice Activity
    ↓
Review AI-Generated Questions
    ↓
Customize the Activity
    ↓
Preview the Student Experience
    ↓
Publish the Practice
    ↓
Monitor Group Progress
    ↓
Review Individual Student Progress
    ↓
Prepare the Next Learning Activity
```

### Navigation Principles

Teachers should always be able to:

- return to the dashboard without losing their work;
- pause and resume unfinished tasks;
- access student information quickly;
- navigate between groups with minimal effort.

The interface should prioritize instructional efficiency.

---

# Student Journey

The student experience is intentionally simple, focused, and distraction-free.

Students should spend their time learning—not navigating.

## Primary Workflow

```text
Landing Page
    ↓
Enter Student Access Code
    ↓
Student Dashboard
    ↓
Continue Current Practice
        or
Choose a Recommended Activity
    ↓
AI Tutoring Session
    ↓
Personalized Learning Summary
    ↓
Return to Student Dashboard
```

### Navigation Principles

Students should always understand:

- where they are;
- what they are learning;
- what remains to be completed;
- what they should do next.

Whenever possible, interrupted learning sessions should be recoverable.

---

# Secondary Learning Flows

In addition to the primary journeys, Socrato supports complementary learning experiences.

Examples include:

- Review by Theme;
- Review by Intellectual Operation;
- Teacher-Assigned Practice;
- Free Practice;
- Student Progress Dashboard;
- Teacher Preview Mode;
- End-of-Year Learning Summary.

These workflows should integrate naturally into the primary navigation without creating parallel or disconnected user experiences.

---

# Navigation Principles

Every navigation decision should follow the same principles.

## Keep the Next Action Clear

Users should never hesitate about what to do next.

Every screen should naturally guide users toward the next educational step.

---

## Minimize Steps

Educational objectives should require the fewest reasonable interactions.

Complex workflows should be simplified whenever possible.

---

## Preserve Context

Changing pages should never unnecessarily interrupt learning or instructional work.

Users should always be able to resume their task without losing context.

---

## Make Recovery Easy

Mistakes happen.

Users should always be able to return safely to previous screens without losing progress or information.

---

## Stay Consistent

Navigation patterns should remain predictable throughout the application.

Consistency reduces cognitive effort and builds confidence.

---

## Keep Learning at the Center

Navigation is a means—not an objective.

Every navigation decision should help users reach educational goals more efficiently.

---

## Navigation Check

Before introducing or modifying navigation, ask:

- Does the user always know what to do next?
- Is the workflow as short as reasonably possible?
- Can interrupted work be resumed easily?
- Is navigation consistent with the rest of the platform?
- Does navigation reduce cognitive load?
- Does it help users stay focused on learning?

If the answer is **No**, the navigation should be redesigned before implementation.

## Purpose of AI

Artificial intelligence is the educational engine of Socrato.

AI is a learning facilitator—not the product itself.

Every interaction should contribute to meaningful educational progress.

## Teach, Don't Answer

The AI should guide students toward understanding rather than immediately providing solutions.

Whenever educationally appropriate, responses should rely on:

- guiding questions;
- progressive hints;
- constructive feedback;
- scaffolded explanations;

The objective is to develop independent learners—not dependence on AI.

---

Every interaction should adapt to the individual learner.

The AI should continuously consider:

- demonstrated understanding;
- previous progress;
- misconceptions;
- confidence;
- learning pace.

Support should remain appropriately challenging without becoming discouraging.

---

## Follow the Teacher's Intent

Teachers define the educational context.

AI behavior should always remain aligned with:

- learning objectives;
- classroom activities;
- teacher-created practices;
- instructional priorities.

Artificial intelligence complements teaching—it never overrides it.

---

## Encourage Critical Thinking

The AI should encourage students to:

- analyze evidence;
- justify reasoning;
- compare ideas;
- interpret information;
- reflect before answering.

The objective is to develop reasoning skills rather than simply produce correct answers.

---

## Be Clear and Supportive

Responses should always be:

- accurate;
- concise;
- encouraging;
- age-appropriate;
- respectful.

Feedback should build confidence while correcting misunderstandings.

Students should feel guided—not judged.

---

AI interactions should remain focused on the current educational objective.

AI behavior should evolve through:

- classroom observations;

- Does this encourage independent thinking?
- Does it strengthen learning rather than replace it?
- Is it aligned with the teacher's objectives?

# 8. Non-Goals

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Defining what Socrato is **not** is just as important as defining what it is.

---

## Purpose

Every successful product has clear boundaries.

Without them, new features gradually accumulate until the platform becomes more complex, less focused, and less effective.

The following use cases fall outside the mission of Socrato and should not influence product direction.

---

## Socrato Is Not...

### A Homework Completion Tool

Socrato exists to help students learn—not to complete assignments on their behalf.

The platform should guide students toward understanding rather than providing ready-made answers.

---

### A General-Purpose Chatbot

Socrato is not designed for open-ended conversation or entertainment.

Every interaction should remain connected to a meaningful educational objective.

---

### An Answer Generator

The objective is not to deliver answers as quickly as possible.

The objective is to develop reasoning, understanding, and independent thinking through guided learning.

---

### A Replacement for Teachers

Teachers remain responsible for instruction, assessment, and educational decisions.

Socrato supports teachers by extending personalized learning beyond the classroom.

It does not replace professional judgment.

---

### A Student Surveillance Platform

Socrato is not designed to monitor, track, or evaluate students beyond what is necessary to support learning.

Only educationally meaningful information should be collected.

Student privacy always takes precedence over unnecessary analytics.

---

### A General Artificial Intelligence Assistant

Socrato is not intended to answer every possible question.

Its purpose is to provide educational support within the scope of approved learning activities.

---

## Product Boundaries

Future features should strengthen one or more of the following objectives:

- improve learning;
- support teachers;
- personalize instruction;
- encourage critical thinking;
- strengthen long-term understanding.

Features that do not reinforce these objectives should generally be considered outside the scope of the platform.

---

## Non-Goals Check

Before introducing a new feature, ask:

- Does this reinforce Socrato's educational mission?
- Does it help students learn?
- Does it strengthen the teacher's role?
- Does it remain focused on education?
- Does it avoid unnecessary product complexity?

If the answer is **No**, the feature should not become part of Socrato.

# 9. Decision Hierarchy

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Not all principles have equal priority.
>
> When trade-offs are unavoidable, decisions should follow a consistent hierarchy that protects Socrato's educational mission.

---

## Purpose

Throughout the development of Socrato, situations will arise where two legitimate objectives appear to conflict.

For example:

- privacy versus analytics;
- performance versus simplicity;
- development speed versus code quality;
- new features versus long-term maintainability.

This hierarchy defines which principles always take precedence when compromises become necessary.

---

# Decision Priority

The following priorities should always be respected, from highest to lowest.

| Priority | Principle | Why it comes first |
|----------|-----------|--------------------|
| **1** | Student Learning | Improving learning is the primary purpose of Socrato. |
| **2** | Student Safety | A safe learning environment is essential for every student. |
| **3** | Teacher Experience | Teachers remain the educational experts and primary users of the platform. |
| **4** | Student Privacy | Trust depends on protecting personal information. |
| **5** | Product Simplicity | Simplicity improves usability, learning, and maintainability. |
| **6** | Reliability & Performance | The platform should remain responsive and dependable. |
| **7** | Development Convenience | Convenience for developers should never outweigh educational or ethical priorities. |

---

## Applying the Hierarchy

When two objectives conflict, the higher-ranked principle always prevails.

For example:

- A feature that improves analytics but weakens privacy should be redesigned.
- A shortcut that simplifies development but reduces maintainability should be avoided.
- A visually attractive interface that distracts students from learning should be simplified.

The hierarchy exists to make difficult decisions predictable and consistent across the entire project.

---

## Shared Responsibility

Every contributor is responsible for applying this hierarchy, including:

- software engineers;
- designers;
- product managers;
- AI coding agents;
- future contributors.

No implementation should intentionally violate a higher-priority principle in order to optimize a lower-priority one.

---

## Decision Check

Before making an important product, engineering, or architectural decision, ask:

1. Does this improve student learning?
2. Does it preserve student safety?
3. Does it strengthen the teacher's experience?
4. Does it protect student privacy?
5. Is the solution as simple as possible?
6. Will it remain reliable and maintainable over time?
7. Am I choosing this approach only because it is easier to implement?

If a lower-priority objective compromises a higher-priority one, the decision should be reconsidered.

# 10. Technical Architecture

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> The architecture of Socrato should remain understandable, modular, secure, and capable of evolving for many years without requiring fundamental redesign.

---

## Architectural Philosophy

The architecture exists to support the educational mission of Socrato.

Technical decisions should simplify future development while protecting reliability, security, privacy, and maintainability.

Good architecture is not measured by complexity.

It is measured by clarity.

---

## Modular by Design

Every major responsibility should be isolated within its own module.

Modules should have:

- a single responsibility;
- clearly defined boundaries;
- limited dependencies;
- reusable interfaces.

Changes inside one module should have minimal impact on the rest of the platform.

---

## Separation of Concerns

Different responsibilities should remain clearly separated.

Examples include:

- user interface;
- business logic;
- AI orchestration;
- database access;
- authentication;
- infrastructure services.

Mixing responsibilities should be avoided whenever possible.

---

## API-First Architecture

Application services should communicate through well-defined APIs.

Business logic should remain independent of the user interface whenever practical.

This approach allows future support for:

- web applications;
- mobile applications;
- administrative tools;
- third-party integrations.

The interface should consume services—not contain business rules.

---

## Security by Default

Security is a foundational architectural requirement.

Every layer of the platform should integrate appropriate protection, including:

- authentication;
- authorization;
- encryption;
- secure communication;
- input validation;
- secret management.

Security should never be postponed until later phases of development.

---

## Privacy by Design

Only the minimum educational information required by the platform should be collected and stored.

Whenever possible:

- student identities should remain anonymized;
- AI interactions should avoid personally identifiable information;
- educational data should remain isolated from identity data.

Privacy requirements should influence architecture from the beginning—not after implementation.

---

## Scalability

The platform should grow naturally from:

- a single classroom;
- a single school;
- multiple school districts;
- province-wide deployment.

Scaling should require additional infrastructure—not architectural redesign.

---

## Reliability and Resilience

Failures should be anticipated rather than assumed impossible.

The platform should gracefully handle:

- temporary service failures;
- network interruptions;
- API timeouts;
- unexpected user behavior.

Learning sessions should be protected whenever possible.

---

## Performance

Performance contributes directly to learning.

Students and teachers should experience fast, responsive interactions throughout the platform.

Performance optimizations should focus on:

- efficient architecture;
- appropriate caching;
- optimized queries;
- thoughtful API usage.

Premature optimization should be avoided.

---

## Observability

Healthy systems are observable systems.

The platform should expose meaningful:

- logs;
- metrics;

# 13. Coding Standards

## Coding Philosophy

Readable code is easier to review, debug, test, and extend.

- understand;
- reuse;
- test;
- maintain;
- extend.

- naming;
- formatting;
- project organization;
- architectural patterns;
- error handling;

reduce cognitive load for every contributor.

Personal coding preferences should never outweigh project consistency.

---

## Prefer Simplicity

Avoid unnecessary abstraction, premature optimization, and overly complex patterns.

Complexity should exist only when it provides measurable value.

---

## Reuse Before Rebuilding

Before creating new code, verify whether an existing implementation already solves the problem.

Prefer:

- extending;
- composing;
- reusing;

over duplicating functionality.

Every duplicate implementation increases long-term maintenance costs.

---

## Separate Concerns

Different responsibilities should remain independent.

Examples include:

- presentation;
- business logic;
- data access;
- AI orchestration;
- infrastructure;
- utilities.

Clear separation improves testing, scalability, and maintainability.

## Write Defensive Code

- invalid input;
- missing data;
- API failures;
- network interruptions;
- unexpected user behavior.

Failure handling is part of good software design.

---

## Design for Maintainability

Every implementation should remain understandable years after it is written.

Future developers should be able to modify the system confidently without introducing regressions.

Maintainable code reduces technical debt and accelerates future development.

---

## Test Critical Functionality

Business-critical logic should be validated through appropriate testing.

Testing increases confidence—not bureaucracy.

---

## Leave the Codebase Better

Every contribution should improve the project.

- clearer code;
- better naming;
- improved documentation;
- reduced duplication;
- simplified logic;
- stronger tests.

- readable;
- documented when appropriate;
- maintainable;

- reviewed before acceptance.

# 16. Git Workflow

> **Guiding Principle**
>
> Version control protects the integrity of the project.
>
> Every commit should make the history of Socrato easier to understand, review, reproduce, and maintain.

---

## Workflow Philosophy

Git is more than a version control system.

It is the permanent history of how Socrato evolves.

Every branch, commit, merge, and release should contribute to a clear, traceable, and reliable development process.

A clean Git history is a form of documentation.

---

## Protect the Main Branch

The `main` branch should always contain:

- stable code;
- tested code;
- deployable code.

Direct development on `main` should be avoided.

All changes should be introduced through reviewed branches.

---

## Use Focused Branches

Every change should have its own dedicated branch.

Typical examples include:

```text
feature/student-dashboard
feature/practice-generator
feature/group-management

fix/access-code-validation
fix/mobile-layout

refactor/ai-service
refactor/database-layer

docs/engineering-handbook
docs/technical-specifications
```

Each branch should pursue one clearly defined objective.

---

## Make Small, Meaningful Commits

Each commit should represent one logical change.

Avoid commits that combine unrelated modifications.

Smaller commits make reviews, debugging, and rollbacks significantly easier.

---

## Write Clear Commit Messages

Commit messages should explain **what changed**, not how difficult it was to implement.

Examples:

```text
Add student access code validation

Improve teacher dashboard navigation

Refactor AI tutoring service

Update Engineering Handbook
```

Good commit messages improve the long-term readability of the repository.

---

## Review Before Committing

Before every commit:

- review modified files;
- remove temporary code;
- remove debugging statements;
- verify formatting;
- verify documentation updates;
- ensure no secrets or personal information are included.

Every commit should be something you would confidently publish.

---

## Test Before Merging

Branches should only be merged after appropriate validation.

Validation may include:

- manual testing;
- automated tests;
- accessibility verification;
- code review;
- regression checks.

The objective is to protect the stability of the `main` branch.

---

## Keep Pull Requests Focused

Each pull request should pursue one objective.

A pull request should explain:

- its purpose;
- implementation summary;
- testing performed;
- potential impacts.

Smaller pull requests are easier to review and safer to merge.

---

## Resolve Conflicts Carefully

Merge conflicts should never be resolved automatically without understanding both versions.

Every conflict represents an architectural decision that deserves careful review.

Preserving code quality is more important than resolving conflicts quickly.

---

## Preserve Traceability

Whenever practical, important commits should reference:

- the related feature;
- architectural decisions;
- documentation updates;
- future implementation notes.

Future contributors should understand both **what changed** and **why**.

---

## Never Commit Sensitive Information

The repository must never contain:

- API keys;
- passwords;
- secrets;
- environment files;
- credentials;
- personally identifiable student information.

Security begins with disciplined development practices.

- deployments;
- rollbacks;
- debugging;
- historical traceability.
